Describe only diegetic sound, in two layers. Continuous ambience: the place’s steady sound bed (rain on the glass, distant traffic, wind in the trees, the hum of a refrigerator), written as persisting through the whole shot, including pauses and silences between actions, so the shot never falls to dead silence. Action effects: sounds produced by the actions written in action, and only those (footsteps on wet stone as she walks, the rustle of a coat as he turns, a cup set down on the table), each tied to the visible action that makes it and occurring with it; do not invent sounds for actions the shot does not show. Spoken words never go here: speech belongs to this mode’s own dialogue or narration channel, and narration or off-screen voice is added in post-production. Do not write BGM, musical scores, or music cues, including mood wording that implies a score (such as “tense strings” or “an uplifting soundtrack”); score is placed on the edit timeline, not generated in clips.
   Good: “Steady rain patters on the window and a radiator hums throughout; the letter paper rustles as her fingers stroke its edge, and the chair creaks once as she leans back.” A persistent ambience bed plus two effects, each tied to a visible action.
   Bad: “Tense music swells as she remembers the past; a man’s voice whispers her name.” Music is score, not diegetic sound; the voice is speech without a speaker in the shot.
